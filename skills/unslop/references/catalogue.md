# Spotting slop, then fixing it without creating more

"Design slop" is not a style but an **absence of decision**: the interface, image or video reproduces the statistical average of what the tool has already seen (indigo gradient, Inter, three identical cards, waxy skin, one-second cuts). "UX slop" is its functional counterpart: a product built for the demo, whose empty, loading and error states do not exist. The visual signs are now widely agreed upon and covered by executable catalogues (Impeccable, Hallmark, pixelslop, taste-skill), but their evidentiary value stays low: they are matters of taste, often defensible in isolation. UX and accessibility defects, by contrast, rest on standards and measurements (95.9% of home pages fail an automated WCAG test, 67% of sites remove the focus outline, ChatGPT inserts an average of five unrequested dark patterns into a generated shop). The most useful finding for a tool is that **anti-slop fixes themselves become a house style**: the cream + terracotta + italic serif wave that 2025 guides recommended is already banned by the current version of Anthropic's skill. An anti-slop skill must therefore measure convergence toward defaults and dated attractors, accept written brand exceptions, treat accessibility and states as blockers, and treat taste as warnings only. This report provides the rule catalogue, the evidence levels, the gaps, and a skill architecture compatible with a machine that supports only one headless browser.

## Five takeaways

**1. Visual slop boils down to a few ecosystem defaults.** The purple/indigo gradient (eleven independent sources), Inter as the only font (ten), the icon + title + text triple card (seven), a large `border-radius` everywhere and the same soft shadow (eight each) trace back to Tailwind (`bg-indigo-500`), shadcn/ui + Lucide templates and the section order of SaaS templates ([prg.sh](https://prg.sh/ramblings/Why-Your-AI-Keeps-Building-the-Same-Purple-Gradient-Website), [Anthropic](https://www.claude.com/blog/improving-frontend-design-through-skills)). Anthropic calls the mechanism "distributional convergence".

**2. UX slop is the missing unhappy path.** Empty states, endless loading, silent failures, double submission and lost input are described by every teardown of "vibe-coded" apps, but only by agencies that sell cleanup, with no prevalence data ([Sinton](https://www.sinton.agency/blog/how-to-spot-a-vibe-coded-web-app), [Digitraly](https://www.digitraly.com/vibe-coded-app-ux-why-users-can-tell-and-what-to-fix/)). It is detected mostly by forcing unfavorable conditions: empty database, slow network, 500 response, double click, reload mid-flow, 320 px screen.

**3. Accessibility is the only mature, standards-based layer.** Six error types account for 96% of detected failures ([WebAIM Million 2026](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)), axe-core (MPL-2.0) finds them with no claimed false positives, and LLM-generated interfaces add a semantic layer (alt `image`, generic labels) that syntactic scanners let through ([Calo et al., CHI EA '26](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3799364)).

**4. Fixes become the new slop.** "Tasteslop" (italic serif, Instrument Serif, cream `#F4F1EA`, terracotta `#D97757`, letter-spaced uppercase labels, "→" arrow) was born partly from the anti-slop instructions themselves ([Anthropic's SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). A frozen blocklist goes stale within months.

**5. The right architecture already exists in pieces.** A skill with dependency-free scripts (regex on code, a single headless Chrome instance driven over CDP, `getComputedStyle` probe + injected axe-core, serial screenshots, visual critique capped at three rounds) beats an MCP server on context cost and load control ([Playwright MCP](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp), [agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/specification)).

## How to read the catalogue

Each rule has a stable ID: `UI-` for interface visuals, `COPY-` for interface copy, `VIS-` for images, social visuals and videos, `UX-` for flows and states, `A11Y-` for accessibility, `DARK-` for dark patterns and `CONV-` for convergence rules, which detect slop without a fixed list. The **Detection** column uses five methods: *static* (grep or AST on JSX, CSS, HTML), *DOM* (rendered DOM and computed styles, axe-core), *interaction* (keyboard, clicks, network interception, viewport changes), *vision* (screenshot + vision model) and *human*. **Severity** measures impact, independently of evidence: **blocking** means anything that prevents a task, breaks a flow, loses data, deceives the user, ships a visible defect, or fails a testable WCAG A/AA criterion that hinders use; a **warning** degrades the experience or credibility; a **suggestion** is a matter of taste or convergence. Purely aesthetic signs are therefore never blocking, except placeholder content shipped to production and visible image defects. The **evidence level** is a separate column, and the skill must combine the two: a blocking rule backed by a single source is presented as a question to the user, not as a failure. The **evidence level** distinguishes *consensus* (at least three independent sources), *supported* (two sources, or one research source or standard) and *single source / unverified*. Counts follow those in the research notes, which already discard derivative sources (codemyspec quotes Anthropic word for word; 925studios and noqta repeat the prg.sh story). Two caveats apply to the whole catalogue: Impeccable and taste-skill postdate Anthropic's skill and reuse its vocabulary, so part of their agreement may be inherited rather than independent; and the UX sources on vibe-coded apps are agency blogs.

## Interface visuals converge on five Tailwind and shadcn defaults

The first generation of signs is documented from October 2025 to November 2026 with remarkable consistency, and several catalogues already translate it into verifiable CSS values. The only prevalence figure available comes from Adrian Krebs, who scored Show HN pages against his own patterns: **22% high scores and 32% medium scores**, which he calls "uninspired" rather than a problem in itself ([Krebs](https://adriankrebs.ch/blog/design-slop/)). It is a single author's measurement with his own grid; it gives an order of magnitude, not a prevalence.

## Interface copy betrays the absence of a real product

Copy signs mirror the visual ones: interchangeable claims, round numbers, fictional names. The cheapest test is to replace the product name with a competitor's; if the headline and figures stay true, they say nothing. This test extends Marty Neumeier's "logo swap test", known only from secondhand accounts ([Brand Autopsy](https://brandautopsy.com/2011/06/the-logo-test-in-action.html)). The most serious part is not stylistic: Krauß et al. show that ChatGPT adds testimonials (11 cases) and fake discounts (18 cases) on its own to pages generated from neutral business prompts ([CHI '25](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03108v2)). In generated code, "filler content" and "dark pattern" can therefore describe the same artifact.

Two copy rules depend on the language and must go through a `fr-FR` profile. Vercel's guidelines require Title Case on headings and buttons, curly English quotation marks and the `…` ellipsis ([Vercel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md)); a French interface uses sentence case (the position of [GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)), « » guillemets and non-breaking spaces before « : ; ! ? ». These French rules are not sourced in the notes: they come from project conventions. Likewise, the French transposition of the GOV.UK blacklist (« Veuillez… », « Désolé », « Oups », « invalide », « interdit », « Vous avez oublié », « Une erreur est survenue » with no next step) is **proposed, not sourced**, and the em dash is an AI sign only beyond a certain density, since French typography legitimately uses it for parenthetical asides. All of these French rules apply only when the project locale is French (`fr-FR` in `.anti-slop.json`).

## Images and videos shift from visible defects to uniformity

For generated visuals, the signs drift from defects (fingers, unreadable text) toward uniformity (smoothing, saturation, recurring styles and subjects). Hany Farid (UC Berkeley) explains that most models apply an "aesthetic" filter on input and output, hence "bright and saturated" colors, "beautiful" people and surfaces smoothed "like the icing on a wedding cake" ([Berkeley](https://www.ischool.berkeley.edu/news/2024/certain-ethereal-quality-hany-farid-explains-why-ai-art-looks-way-it-does)). The taxonomy of Kamali et al. sorts artifacts into anatomical, stylistic, functional, physical and sociocultural, and warns that perceptible artifacts "are not always present" ([arXiv 2406.08651](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.08651)). Automatically detecting an "AI" image is therefore not the goal: the tool must spot what costs trust. The data supports this. In the NIQ study (over 2,000 participants, about 150 of them under EEG), AI ads come across as more "annoying, boring, confusing" and activate memory less even when judged to be of good quality ([NIQ](https://nielseniq.com/global/en/news-center/2024/niq-research-uncovers-hidden-consumer-attitudes-toward-ai-generated-ads/)), while content merely suspected of being AI loses about 48% of trust at Raptive, on text articles ([PPC Land](https://ppc.land/raptive-study-shows-ai-content-cuts-reader-trust-by-half/)). Conversely, System1 gave Coca-Cola's AI Christmas ad the top score of 5.9 among viewers who had not been told ([System1](https://system1group.com/ad-of-the-week/ai-or-no-ai-coke-gets-the-christmas-love)): the penalty owes as much to perceived origin as to defects.

## UX slop hides in the states the demo never showed

The most common explanation is mechanical: the generator builds the path the prompt describes, hence the happy path, and nothing else ([Digitraly](https://www.digitraly.com/vibe-coded-app-ux-why-users-can-tell-and-what-to-fix/)); Kosta Canatselis sums it up as a model that "optimizes for the demo" ([Kosta Canatselis](https://world.hey.com/kostac/spot-the-slop-a-ui-designer-s-guide-to-fixing-ai-defaults-4c448c9c)). The weakness of this family lies in its sources: **no quantitative audit of the UX of vibe-coded apps exists**. The published "audits" of Lovable, Bolt or v0 are about security ([dev.to, 50 apps](https://dev.to/rishabh_kumar_6d865c83a4d/i-audited-50-vibe-coded-apps-heres-what-broke-1pb6)), and Afterbuild Labs' report describes itself as a meta-analysis of testimonials ([Afterbuild](https://afterbuildlabs.com/resources/state-of-vibe-coding-2026)). Several rules are still classed as blocking because they match a standard or a measured canon: NN/g for error messages and response times, Baymard for validation (31% of e-commerce sites with no inline validation, 4% that do it badly) ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)), GOV.UK and WCAG for submission. The resulting method is to **force unfavorable conditions** rather than read the code: empty database, response delayed by 3 then 12 s, 500 response, offline mode, double click, reload mid-flow, back button, 320 px viewport.

## Accessibility gives the skill its only indisputable blockers

Accessibility is the only layer where prevalence is measured at scale and thresholds are standardized. In 2026, **95.9% of top-million home pages fail** an automated WCAG test, with 56.1 errors per page on average (+10.1% year over year), and six error types account for 96% of the total ([WebAIM Million](https://webaim.org/projects/million/)). Page complexity grows **14.3%** in a year, and pages that use ARIA have more errors (59.1 vs 42); this correlation advises an automatic fixer to prefer native elements (`<button>`, `<dialog>`, `<label>`) over adding roles. Generated interfaces add a semantic layer: across 300 interfaces produced by Claude Sonnet-4, Gemini-2.5-flash and GPT-4o, Calo et al. find 541 violations (alt `image`, generic labels, headings unrelated to their section), from 0.3 to 1.8 per interface depending on the model; it is an extended abstract whose judgment was made by a validated LLM ([CHI EA '26](https://doi.org/10.1145/3772363.3799364)). React and Next.js versions nonetheless show fewer errors than average (−22.5% and −27.1%): generated apps probably pass axe better than average while failing on meaning, which requires pairing axe, interaction tests and model judgment. AAA criteria remain advisory: 2.3.3 (animation from interactions) and 2.4.13 (focus appearance) are never AA failures. For a French audit, RGAA 4.1.2 reuses the same thresholds (criteria 3.2, 3.3, 10.7, 11.1; wording to verify) ([RGAA](https://accessibilite.numerique.gouv.fr/methode/criteres-et-tests/)).

## Dark patterns arrive without being asked for

The most underestimated risk of a generated app is that it manipulates by default. The ICPEN sweep of 642 subscription sites and apps (January–February 2024) finds at least one possible dark pattern on **nearly 76%** of them, and several on nearly 67%, led by information concealment and interface interference ([FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy)). Krauß et al. asked 20 participants to have ChatGPT build a fictional shop with neutral business goals: **all 20 final pages contained at least one dark pattern, five on average**, mostly visual prominence (21 cases), fake discounts (18), testimonials (11) and limited-time messages (11), and the model warned the user only once out of twenty; preliminary tests on Claude 3.5 Sonnet and Gemini 1.5 Flash point the same way ([CHI '25](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.03108v2)). Any pricing, checkout, sign-up or cancellation page produced by a model must therefore be presumed suspect until verified. The definitions follow the [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types) taxonomy; the detection methods are proposed by the notes, not by this source. Proving a claim false requires data or a human: the tool flags and asks, it does not delete.

| ID | Sign | Fix | Detection | Severity | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| DARK-09 | Costs revealed late in the checkout flow | Total price from the product page | Interaction in test mode (compare displayed price and total before payment) | Blocker | Consensus: [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy) (most frequent category), [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types) |
| DARK-10 | Unannounced subscription, buried cancellation | "per month, renews automatically" next to the CTA; cancellation in settings in as many steps as signing up | Interaction (count screens to subscribe and to cancel); static | Blocker | Supported: [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types), [FTC](https://ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/07/ftc-icpen-gpen-announce-results-review-use-dark-patterns-affecting-subscription-services-privacy) |
| DARK-11 | Nagging (modal or permission request that returns after being closed, notification requested on load) and forced action (account required before any value) | One request, at the moment it makes sense; public content reachable without an account | Interaction (close, reload, change route); DOM (`Notification.requestPermission` without an action) | Warning | Supported: [deceptive.design](https://deceptive.design/types), [NN/g, modals](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/modal-nonmodal-dialog/) |

A fix that rewrites conversion copy with an LLM can reintroduce these patterns. The conclusion of Krauß et al. applies to the fixer itself: any copy generated by the skill must pass the DARK checks again before being proposed.

## Designers and AI practitioners give the same advice: decide before styling

The best-supported advice comes from older sources and owes nothing to AI. Michael Bierut (Pentagram) studies the building and history of MAD before drawing, and finds the solution in the geometry of the letters themselves (as read from a 2015 account) ([The Creative Store](https://thecreativestore.co.nz/creative-talk/design/how-to-think-like-michael-bierut/)); Dan Mall reads the text before choosing a typeface ([Dan Mall](https://danmall.com/learn/process-craft/how-i-choose-typefaces/)); Dan Rhatigan reminds us that software default fonts decide the outcome before the work begins ([AIGA Eye on Design](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/why-default-fonts-lead-to-default-work-for-everyone/)); PostHog targets a single audience and derives every visual choice from it ([PostHog](https://posthog.com/blog/brand)). John Davenport gives the most useful formula for a tool: "Conventional is fine. Visibly undecided is not." ([codemyspec](https://codemyspec.com/blog/vibe-coded-websites-look-the-same)).

### What is agreed and what remains opinion

The consensus (at least three independent sources) comes down to six points. Start from the problem, the content and the audience before the style (Bierut, Rhatigan, Mall, PostHog). Choose typography deliberately (Rhatigan and Butterick, Mall, Davenport). Look for references outside the field and outside "like"-driven platforms: Elizabeth Goodspeed builds her own archive and warns against Pinterest and Are.na ([It's Nice That](https://itsnicethat.com/articles/elizabeth-goodspeed-column-taste-technology-art-280224)), Sahadeva Hammari describes a "performative design" calibrated for Dribbble ([AIGA](https://eyeondesign.aiga.org/todays-design-is-shaped-by-likes-and-thats-a-problem/)), Paul Graham urges studying what other disciplines have learned ([Taste for Makers](http://www.paulgraham.com/taste.html)). Subtract: Dieter Rams's "Less, but better" ([Vitsoe](https://www.vitsoe.com/us/about/good-design)). Learn taste through exposure and volume (Goodspeed, Graham, Ira Glass). Use real content instead of lorem ipsum from mid-fidelity on (UXmatters, uxdesign.cc and The Next Web, read only in excerpts). Opposing this is an equally solid counter-consensus: conventions serve usability. Matt Ström explains uniformity through Hotelling's principle of minimum differentiation, each team making the least risky choice ([Sameification](https://mattstromawn.com/writing/sameification/)), and Kate Moran (NN/g) points out that simplicity does not require uniformity but that anti-design fails for most products ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/brutalism-antidesign/)). The synthesis that reconciles both camps applies directly to a tool: **keep navigation, forms and flows conventional, and spend singularity on concept, content, typography, imagery and voice.**


Single-source items, by contrast: Neumeier's logo swap test (2003, known from secondhand accounts), Dan Mall's 4-to-100 typeface audition, PostHog's rejection of the 80/20 rule for branding, Figma's six critique formats ([Figma](https://www.figma.com/blog/design-critiques-at-figma/)), NN/g's question "does this change serve the user or your boredom?" and the identifiability test ("can you guess which generator produced the app?") from [Robots on Payroll](https://robotsonpayroll.substack.com/p/how-to-avoid-that-vibe-coded-look). Two practices are supported by two 2026 blogs: write design decisions once, in a single file, then enforce them (Davenport, Robots on Payroll); and the squint test for hierarchy (NN/g, Polypane, in excerpts).

### AI vendors converge on explicit context

On the tooling side, Anthropic, OpenAI, Vercel and Lovable make the same diagnosis (the model samples the center of the distribution) and prescribe the same remedy (more project-specific context). Seven techniques recur ([Anthropic cookbook](https://platform.claude.com/cookbook/coding-prompting-for-frontend-aesthetics), [OpenAI GPT-5.4](https://developers.openai.com/blog/designing-delightful-frontends-with-gpt-5-4), [v0](https://v0.app/docs/design-systems), [Lovable](https://docs.lovable.dev/features/design-guidance)): ban the defaults **while naming the replacements**, name a concrete aesthetic direction, fix the tokens or design system before any generation, provide screenshots or references, use real content, give the model a browser so it can see what it produced, and set restraint rules ("Spend your boldness in one place", a single orchestrated motion moment, "no cards by default"). The first four and the screenshot loop are agreed on; "low or medium reasoning effort yields better frontends" is claimed only by OpenAI. v0 adds a decisive constraint for a fixer: its model is trained on default shadcn/ui components and "may struggle with any customizations", so it is better to re-theme through tokens than to rewrite base components ([v0](https://v0.app/docs/design-systems)). Finally, the Claude Code documentation settles the question of the rules file: CLAUDE.md and skills are followed non-deterministically, and a rule that must never be broken belongs in a hook or a check, not in more insistent wording ([Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/best-practices)). None of these recommendations has been evaluated in a controlled test: the evidence is before/after galleries and testimonials.

## Anti-slop fixes become the new house style

The finding that weighs most on the tool's design is chronological. In October 2025, Anthropic's cookbook recommended Playfair Display, Crimson Pro and Fraunces for "editorial", the display + monospace pairing and backgrounds "with atmosphere", while admitting that the model was already reconverging on Space Grotesk ([cookbook](https://platform.claude.com/cookbook/coding-prompting-for-frontend-aesthetics)). The current version of the `frontend-design` skill now lists as AI signs to avoid: the cream background near `#F4F1EA` with a high-contrast serif and a terracotta accent near `#D97757`, near-black with a single acid green or vermilion accent, the "broadsheet" layout with hairline rules and zero radius, the "SaaS-card kit", letter-spaced uppercase labels, middle-dot meta strings, monospace labels and the arrow appended to links ([SKILL.md](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/anthropics/skills/main/skills/frontend-design/SKILL.md)). In the meantime, Wired (relayed by AI Weekly, article not read) describes Instrument Serif as the default rendering of the vibe-coded web ([AI Weekly](https://aiweekly.co/alerts/ai-has-come-for-serif-fonts)), Xataka popularizes the term "tasteslop" ([Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/robotica-e-ia/ultimo-truco-empresas-ia-para-parecer-artificiales-usar-tipografias-clasicas-llegada-tasteslop/amp)), and the look of Claude.ai circulates as a ready-made DESIGN.md template ([shadcn.io](https://www.shadcn.io/design/claude)). The serif wave also has its own cause: AI companies (Claude, Perplexity, Manus, Runway) adopt serifs to seem human, and templates copy their look. That the cream + terracotta palette recalls Claude's brand is an observation from the notes, **unverified**.

Anti-slop tools acknowledge this mechanism themselves. taste-skill describes itself as applying a "strict no-slop house style" ([taste-skill](https://github.com/Leonxlnx/taste-skill)), and Can Dedeoglu warns that highly prescriptive tools produce an impressive demo while locking every project into the same narrow style ([candede.com](https://www.candede.com/articles/escaping-ai-slop-in-web-design)). Copying the DESIGN.md of a famous brand (Stripe, Linear, Claude) trades generic slop for clone slop, acceptable for an internal tool and weak for a brand that has to stand out. The conflict shows up in practice: taste-skill's linter fails `mono`, `gradient` and `purple` (local lint.mjs), while the project's style guide uses JetBrains Mono for code, a yellow `#fcd21d` and a `#02021c` background. A tool that applied a fixed list would flag the brand itself.

### Three consequences for the tool

**Detect convergence rather than ban a list.** The underlying sign is not a font or a color, it is the identical choice across unrelated products. The tool therefore compares the project's values with the defaults of its tooling (Tailwind theme, shadcn variables, Lucide at 24 px, template section order) and with a **dated, versioned** list of attractors (2025 generation: indigo, Inter, cards; 2026 generation: cream, italic serif, eyebrows, mono), which must be revised as the distribution shifts.

**Accept written per-project exceptions.** Each exception carries a reason, in the manner of taste-skill's `--allow <rule>` or the `avoid-ai-design-ignore: ID` comments ([avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design)). A justified value stops being a default, even if it appears in the attractor list.

**Expose conflicting rules as profiles.** The sources contradict each other on several points where no winner should be hardcoded (table below).

| Topic | Position A | Position B | Proposed setting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Validation timing | Inline, on leaving the field, error cleared as the user types ([Baymard](https://baymard.com/blog/inline-form-validation)) | On submit; live validation only if research justifies it ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)) | `forms.validation: blur | submit`; common rule: no premature error |
| Disabled button | Disable during the async action (ui-ux-pro-max) | Avoid disabled buttons ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)); Vercel keeps submit enabled until the request leaves | `buttons.disabledWhilePending: true | false` |
| Confirm or undo | Confirm before any deletion (ui-ux-pro-max) | Prefer undo, keep confirmation for serious actions ([NN/g](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/confirmation-dialog/)) | `destructive: undo-first | confirm-first` |
| Focus after failure | First invalid field ([Vercel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md)) | Summary at the top + "Error:" prefix in the title ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/patterns/validation/)) | `forms.errorFocus: first-field | summary` |
| Case and typography | Title Case, English quotes, `…` ([Vercel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/vercel-labs/web-interface-guidelines/main/command.md)) | Sentence case ([GOV.UK](https://design-system.service.gov.uk/components/button/)); French conventions (unsourced) | `locale: fr-FR` disables the English rules and makes COPY-09 language-aware |
| Toast duration | 3 to 5 s (ui-ux-pro-max) | Time limits must be adjustable ([WCAG 2.2.1](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)) | Warning, never blocking |
| Target size | 44 pt, 48 dp + 8 dp gap (Apple, Android) | 24×24 CSS px ([WCAG 2.5.8](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/target-size-minimum.html)) | Blocker below 24 px, warning below 44 px for mobile profiles |

## What the notes do not let us claim

Several gaps remain visible and must stay flagged as such in the skill. **No prevalence study** measures visual slop beyond Krebs's personal scoring, and the same author's "design slop report" returns a 404; pixelslop's severity ratings are authorial choices. **No quantitative UX audit** of vibe-coded apps exists: every "audit" found is about security, and the article comparing Lovable and Bolt (jarbon, Medium) stayed blocked. **No anti-slop linter** (Impeccable, taste-skill, avoid-ai-design) publishes precision or recall; their blind tests are self-reported and judged by models. **No controlled evaluation** shows that DESIGN.md, screenshot loops or anti-slop skills improve perceived quality or user outcomes, and no tool publishes how often its automatic fixes break a feature.

The following remain unverified and must not be coded as rules: "bento" grids as a sign of slop (only component libraries), "Trusted by" banners (excerpts only), the ✨ sparkle as an AI-feature icon outside the NN/g study, the "four horsemen" quote attributed to Wes Bos (a single page signed "AI Bot"), the figure of 84% of ChatGPT sites being inaccessible, the 47% of site captures containing a dark pattern, the "62% correct answers" of the Microsoft quiz, Merriam-Webster's choice of "slop" as word of the year and the statistic "88% of companies use AI design tools". The Montclair study on the Ghibli wave gives two incompatible figures for the share of negative messages ("514.3% more" and "seven to one"); the PDF was not read. On the video and social visuals side, orange-and-teal grading, symmetric compositions, the "Pixar" 3D style, the "swipe →" carousel formula, the "Canva look", Hormozi-style subtitles, stock music and "whoosh" transitions have **no critical source**. On the UX side, optimistic rollback, settings sprawl and the lack of navigation hierarchy are plausible but unsourced; nested cards as a UX defect rest on pages that could not be read, even though the visual catalogues cite them. The French rules (DSFR blocked with a 403, French typography, French error blacklist) are unsourced; the wording of the RGAA criteria needs rechecking. Finally, Reddit, HN and X were hardly read directly: the "practitioner" consensus rests more on catalogues and agencies than it should.

## Building the anti-slop skill

### What to reuse rather than write

Most of it already exists under permissive licenses, and each tool imposes its own style: the work is to assemble and reconfigure, not to invent.

To discard in this context: **Deque Axe MCP**, paid, included in the Axe DevTools offering ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/axe-devtools-for-web-now-includes-axe-mcp-server-for-earlier-fixes-and-faster-delivery/)); **Chrome DevTools MCP** as the main engine, because by default it sends usage statistics and may send URLs to the CrUX API (disabled with `--no-usage-statistics` and `--no-performance-crux`), and would launch a second browser ([GitHub](https://github.com/ChromeDevTools/chrome-devtools-mcp)); **Playwright MCP**, which opens in headed mode by default ([GitHub](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)); and pixel-by-pixel comparisons to detect slop, since Playwright warns that rendering varies with the OS, the hardware and even battery versus mains power ([Playwright](https://playwright.dev/docs/test-snapshots)): they are used only for the before/after step, within the same session. Existing "deslop" skills (brianlovin and others) deal with code, not design, and the name collides with this project.

### A skill with scripts, an MCP only later

The evidence favors a skill. The Agent Skills specification (a folder with `SKILL.md`, `scripts/`, `references/`, about 100 tokens of metadata at startup, a body under 5,000 tokens, resources loaded on demand) is read by Claude Code and by Codex ([agentskills.io](https://agentskills.io/specification), [Codex](https://learn.chatgpt.com/docs/build-skills)); only a script's output enters the context. Playwright MCP's README says it itself: coding agents increasingly prefer CLIs exposed as skills, which are more token-efficient, and keep MCP for loops that need persistent state ([Playwright MCP](https://github.com/microsoft/playwright-mcp)); Anthropic showed that exposing tools as on-demand code takes an example from 150,000 to 2,000 tokens ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/code-execution-with-mcp)). An MCP server is justified in only three cases: a client with no shell (Claude Desktop, ChatGPT), a browser session that must last across many calls (authentication, multi-step flows), or an audit service shared on a remote machine. It would then remain a thin wrapper around the same scripts, and Codex already lets a skill declare an MCP dependency in `agents/openai.yaml`. On a developer laptop, the script is also easier to keep in check than a long-lived browser that an MCP host holds open between calls.

The proposed folder: `SKILL.md` (walkthrough, audit first), `references/` (this report's catalogue, profiles, exception list format, fix guides), `scripts/lint.mjs` (regex), `scripts/inspect.mjs` (one headless Chrome over CDP, probe + axe-core, JSON report and screenshots), a project file `.anti-slop.json` or a section of the DESIGN.md for exceptions and profiles (for example "monospace allowed for code; yellow `#fcd21d` allowed"), and an optional `PostToolUse` hook for the lint. The same folder works in `.claude/skills/` and in `.agents/skills/` with `agents/openai.yaml`, as humanizer does. The fix mode is declared with `disable-model-invocation: true` so that the model does not trigger it on its own ([Claude Code](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/skills)).

### Four detection layers, from cheapest to most expensive

| Layer | When | What it catches | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Static (regex, AST, Biome a11y) | On every edit, no browser | Literal signs: gradient classes, `outline-none`, `onPaste`, `<div onClick>`, lorem ipsum, placeholder names, copy phrases, `href="#"` | Ignores what the cascade decides |
| 2. DOM and computed styles (probe + axe-core) | Once per audit, 2 or 3 widths in series | Real contrast, font inventory, radii, shadows and colors, touch targets, overflow, A11Y rules | Sees neither unrendered states nor meaning |
| 3. Interaction (network interception, keyboard, viewport) | On the main routes and forms | Empty, loading and error states; double submission; lost input; focus; DARK timers | Requires test data and a test environment |
| 4. Vision + model on text | At most one round per page on 2 or 3 screenshots | Hierarchy, focal point, "template feel", relevance of alts and labels | Ranks big problems, does not settle fine choices |

The fourth layer must be bounded by what the research measures. In "MLLM as a UI Judge" (9,296 responses from 500 participants on 30 interfaces), GPT-4o and Claude 3.5 Sonnet reach about 60% pairwise accuracy on average, **close to 90% on very different interfaces and about 50% on similar ones** ([arXiv 2510.08783](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.08783v1)); on 300 real A/B test pairs, the models pick the winner almost at random ([WiserUI-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.05026v4)). The question put to the model must therefore be "is this clearly worse than the reference or the grid?", never "which of these two variants will convert better?". The studies by Duan et al. add that the usefulness of LLM feedback decreases over iterations ([CHI 2024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.13139)) and that the best techniques do not yet match human evaluators ([UICrit](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.08850)).

### The safe fix loop

Existing tools converge on the same sequence (taste-skill, [avoid-ai-design](https://github.com/funboy322/avoid-ai-design), [Impeccable](https://impeccable.style/), [Hallmark](https://github.com/Nutlope/hallmark)), which the skill must adopt without shortcuts. **Measure**: lint and inspection into a `before/` folder. **Report and stop**: JSON report + screenshots, ranked by severity then by evidence, with the three changes that matter most; this is the default mode, which changes nothing. **Get approval**: the user chooses what gets fixed; findings COPY-03, COPY-04 and DARK-01 to DARK-04 are always raised as questions, never deleted or replaced with invented values. **Fix** within the existing stack, with no framework migration, in this order: accessibility and states, then typography, color, framing and components, preferring tokens over components (v0's constraint) and native elements over adding ARIA (WebAIM correlation); any generated copy passes the DARK checks again. **Re-measure** with the same instruments into `after/`, in the same session and on the same machine, click through every link and form that worked again, then present the before/after table and the git diff. **Cap** at three rounds, like taste-skill and in line with the decay measured by Duan et al.; beyond that, the skill hands control back.

### Constraints of a developer machine

Running a dev server, an automated browser and a capture script at the same time can freeze a laptop; the skill's envelope is therefore deliberately small. **A single headless browser** per audit: the Chrome launched by `inspect.mjs`, into which axe-core is injected rather than going through Lighthouse via another MCP. taste-skill's CDP client shows the right practice: `--headless=new`, temporary profile, process closed at the end, cleanup of profiles left by an interrupted run, viewports rendered in series (local cdp.mjs). **Serial screenshots**: one page, one capture, a pause of at least one second; no fast-loop full-page scrolling, and 2 or 3 widths rather than Hallmark's eight. **Reuse the existing dev server** after checking the port (`lsof -iTCP -sTCP:LISTEN -P | grep node`), never launching a second one. **Everything runs through `nice -n 10`**, parallel tools are limited to three workers, and `uptime` is checked before any heavy operation: if the load exceeds the core count, the skill launches nothing and warns. **Stop everything the skill launched** before handing control back. The static lint hook, with no browser, is the only layer that can run on every edit.

## Conclusion

Slop is better defined by what is missing than by what is present: a written decision, real content, an error state, a visible focus. This is why the skill's hierarchy must be inverted relative to the attention the subject receives: the most-discussed visual signs (purple, Inter, cards, serif) are the least probative and deserve only suggestions, while the defects least visible in a demo (missing states, double submission, contrast, dark patterns inserted by the model) are the ones backed by standards and measurements. A tool that did the opposite would produce more "trendy" pages and products that are still broken.

The lesson of tasteslop goes beyond fonts: any fixed grid of taste, this report's included, becomes an attractor once applied at scale. The skill can stay useful only by measuring the gap between a project and the defaults of its era, by dating its lists, by letting the brand write its exceptions, and by leaving to humans the judgments that research shows are beyond the models. The main knowledge gap is also an opportunity: an in-house audit of states and flows on a sample of public Lovable, Bolt or v0 apps would produce the first prevalence measurement of UX slop, which none of the sources provides.
